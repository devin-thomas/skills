# Premium bar for content apps

One hundred and twelve rules an agent can test on the rendered app and its shell (numbered 1 to 112; 41b is the display-level half of 41), and the process around them. Rules 104 to 111 (2026-11-19) close gaps found when the bar was checked against desktop integration, performance architecture, visual QA and production trust; each is built into the starter with a check that fails when the rule is broken (see "Trust, restoration and visual baselines"). The counts of builds below are for rules 1 to 103 and 112. By evidence: 34 rule rows are checked in two builds, 54 in one, 4 in part of one, and 13 in none. On 2026-11-22 the bar was checked against Apple's own text (see "Apple pass" near the end): fifteen rules were reworded to match it and rule 112 (the app icon) was added; no threshold a build measured was changed. Rules 1 to 40 came from craft research; a review against what Linear, Superhuman, Raycast, Stripe, 37signals and the latency literature say changed nine of them and added 41 to 56 and the process steps (round 1, built into the mail app for Windows). Rules 57 to 100 came from round 2, which widened the bar to Windows, macOS and Linux and tested it on the media converter (an Electron app over a command-line tool, on all three systems): five research reports proposed 56 rules, each numbered from 57, and the builders and an independent critic added what testing showed. Rules 101 to 103 came from what the media converter found by experiment after round 2's extraction: why an agent's test window took the owner's focus on Windows, and the largest removable part of its Windows idle memory. "Where the round 2 numbers went" at the end maps every proposal to its number here.

**What passing means.** The rules are a floor that stops known mistakes. Passing every one earns the sentence "passes the automated bar". It does not earn the word premium: that is the owner's verdict after real use (process step 4). In both builds so far the builder's own audit passed with no failures while an independent critic who saw only screenshots found 7 must-fix items. Weigh the groups below in the order they are listed.

**Builds column.** How many builds applied the rule and recorded a check of it: **2** (the mail app and the media converter, both 2026-11-15), **1**, or **0** (proposed by research, not yet built or checked anywhere: a hypothesis). "1 (part)" means one build checked part of it; the rest is unverified. **S** means checked in the three-system starter itself and in no app yet: proven to work and, by `pnpm qa:planted`, to fail when the rule is broken, not yet proven in a product.

**Check column.** Each value names a script in the three-system starter (`starters/web-three-systems`) that fails when the rule fails, or says the check is by hand:

## Process steps

Done in both builds: step 3 (the independent critic). Done in build 2 only: step 1 (journeys written before building) and step 10 (seam files first). Not yet done in any build: steps 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 and 11. They come from the same reviews and are hypotheses.

1. **Name the essential journeys first.** Five to fifteen per app, written before components are chosen, each with its step budget (rule 47). Build 1 named its main journey only after a reviewer counted the steps, and the count removed a whole confirmation step. Build 2 wrote its twelve journeys and shortcut table before any builder started.
2. **Walk the journeys.** A reviewer that did not build the app walks each journey in the real shell with real data and writes a friction log. The owner walks the same journeys and the two are compared. Build 2 ran its journeys by script in the real shell on all three systems; no person walked them.
3. **An independent critic.** The critique agent gets only screenshots and recordings, never the code, the audit or the builder's notes. The builder cannot see the builder's own flaws: in both builds the builder's audit passed with zero failures while the critic found seven must-fix items. Build 1's included implementation words in primary text and an error whose filled button could only fail again; build 2's included errors whose filled button was "Show details", developer text inside Details, a number that overflowed its dial at four digits, a footer that wrapped and crushed the layout at the minimum window, and names cut in the middle. Turn each class of finding into an audit gate so it cannot come back (build 2 did; rules 69, 71, 72, 97 and 98 are the result).
4. **A use gate.** The word premium is not applied until the owner has done real work in the app for a set number of days. Every annoyance in that period goes into the papercut log with a screenshot.
5. **A standing papercut pass.** Each iteration closes at least one flaw that is not a bug, in 30 minutes or less.
6. **Zero known bugs at ship, and a seven-day limit after.**
7. **An appetite and a circuit breaker.** Each screen gets a time budget; when it runs out, scope is cut and the cuts are written down.
8. **Diverge before polishing.** Build three layouts behind a switcher, choose, then run the polish rounds.
9. **Film it once.** One high-speed camera capture per shell and machine, to check the software clocks. It needs the owner's hands or a fixed camera, so ask before planning on it.
10. **Seam files first.** Before parallel builders start, the lead writes the tool contract and the page-to-shell bridge, and nobody else changes them. In build 2 five builders in separate worktrees (tool, adapter, shell, packaging, page) built against the two files and merged with no conflicts.
11. **Native references for the critic.** Give the critic a native app's screenshot per system at the same size beside each app screenshot (Finder or Notes on macOS, Files on GNOME, Dolphin on KDE). Proposed by report 09; build 2's critic saw only the app.

## Apple pass (2026-11-22)

The bar was checked against Apple's Mac and iPhone Human Interface Guidelines, the App Review Guidelines and Apple's web guidance.

Reworded to match Apple: 5 (list rows may show focus as a highlight), 6 (the audit's overlay gate named), 11 (undo naming), 12 and 50 (no error only in a toast), 31 (the 12 px floor kept, recorded as stricter than Apple; semibold at small sizes on macOS), 40 (Reduce Transparency and Increase Contrast together; opaque, not frosted), 46 (each system's own key family), 53 and 81 (menu order with Format, View and Window required, Option-Quit), 70 (completion messages for jobs that write files), 72 (system materials allowed, imitations and decorative effects not), 82 (marked as convention), 92 (no default button on an alert to read), 99 (never bundle SF Pro). Added: 112 (the app icon). Thresholds the builds measured are unchanged: Apple publishes none for durations, bounce, latency or memory except 100 ms for a discrete interaction, which rule 27 already meets.

Apple asks for more that no build has tried, so it is not yet a rule: an increased-contrast variant of every token colour (with 110), scroll position and selection restored on relaunch (with 105), window titles that name the content and never the app, notifications only with consent and never while the app is in front, and drop feedback (a highlighted target, a count on a multi-item drag, a drag from an inactive window that does not activate it; with 88).

What changes when a build goes to the Mac App Store (none of the builds so far has): the build is sandboxed and a bundled tool carries only the `app-sandbox` and `inherit` entitlements (57, 61 to 63, 88); it has no updater, and Electron's store build turns off the crash reporter and may lose some accessibility, so its own audit runs (104, 36, 37, 75); the control channel is compiled out (101, guideline 2.5.19); the start check uses a Development-signed build (87); and social sign-in needs Sign in with Apple beside it, with account deletion in the app (4.8, 5.1.1(v)). Guideline 2.5.6 (WebKit for browsing) does not apply to an app that shows only its own bundled page (106).
